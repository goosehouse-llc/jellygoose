same record will carry iOS, iPadOS and macOS later, and metadata that says
"Apple TV" would have to be rewritten then.

Character counts are the App Store's limits, checked against this text.

JellyGoose is a player for the media server you already run. It finds your Jellyfin server on your network, you pick who's watching, and your films and shows are there: big, poster-forward and quick to move through.

A HOME THAT SHOWS YOU WHAT'S THERE
Whatever you highlight fills the top of the screen with its artwork, logo, tagline and details, while Continue Watching, Next Up and the newest in each library glide into place beneath it.

IT PLAYS WHAT THE SYSTEM PLAYER WON'T
H.264 and HEVC go to Apple's own player, where HDR, Dolby Vision and frame-rate matching belong. AV1, VP9, MPEG-2 and the rest go to a built-in VLC engine, played straight from the original file, so your server never has to re-encode anything.

BUILT FOR BIG FILES
4K remuxes start quickly and stay smooth: a film is made ready while you read its page. TrueHD and DTS reach the Apple TV in full, lossless 7.1. A Stats button shows which player has the film, and whether your server is converting anything and why.

LIVE TV FROM YOUR OWN TUNER
Give your server a tuner, an HDHomeRun for one, and JellyGoose adds Live TV: a guide with your channel playing beside it, what's on now on Home, and recording of one showing or a whole series. ## Trademarks

"Jellyfin" appears in the keywords and the description, where it
describes what the app is compatible with, and not in the app's name, icon or
subtitle. That is nominative use and it is what comparable clients do, but Apple
occasionally objects to third-party marks in metadata. If review queries it,
removing the keywords is a metadata edit, not a new build.

"Seerr" is in 1.3's description, What's New and promotional text on the same
footing: it names what the Requests section works with. It goes into the
keywords with each platform's next version (see "Keywords").

Since build 32 the Requests page is headed by Seerr's logo, the eye and the
word as Seerr's own pages carry them, in place of the word "Requests"; so
the store's Requests screenshot has it too. The file is Seerr's
(`logo_full.svg`, unaltered), Seerr is MIT-licensed, and the app's Licences
page carries its notice and says the two aren't affiliated. A logo is a step
further than a name, all the same. If App Review or the Seerr project minds,
the heading goes back to the word in one place (`RequestsView`) and that
screenshot is taken again.

## Why Emby isn't mentioned

The app connects to Emby too, and on 2026-09-23 it was tried against a real
Emby 4.10 server: sign-in, Home, libraries, film pages, playback, resume and
subtitles all worked. That was before 1.2's new Home and libraries, so the
listing claims Jellyfin alone until 1.2 has had the same pass on Emby. Adding
Emby to the description and keywords then is a metadata edit, not a new build.
