Privacy

Last updated 28 November 2026

JellyGoose collects nothing. There are no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. The app talks to the media server you point it at, and to a Seerr if you connect one, and never to us. There are two exceptions, both described below: while you set it up, it asks your home network where your server is; and if your Seerr doesn't keep its own copies of artwork, the pictures under Requests come from The Movie Database.

What stays on your device

Everything the app remembers is stored on the Apple TV, iPhone, iPad or Mac itself:

  • The address of your server, and the name it reports.
  • The servers you have signed in to before, so you can pick one again without typing its address. You can forget any of them from the setup screen.
  • Your sign-in token, kept in the system keychain. It never leaves the device.
  • Your preferences: colour scheme, app icon, streaming quality, player choice, and any filters you pin to the menu.
  • Anything you download to watch or listen to away from your server.

Deleting the app removes all of it. Signing out deletes the token immediately.

iCloud Sync

Some of the app's own settings, such as how Home and the menu are arranged and your pinned views, are kept in your own iCloud account so your other devices can use them. Apple stores them under its own privacy policy; we can't see them. Your sign-in, your server's address and your watch history are not synced this way. iCloud Sync is on unless you turn it off in Settings.

Finding your server

While you are setting the app up, it asks your local network whether any Jellyfin servers are on it, so you can choose yours instead of typing its address. The question is a single message sent to devices on your own network; any Jellyfin server that hears it replies with its name and address. Nothing about you or your device is included, the message never leaves your home network, and the answers are only used to show the list on screen.

What your server sees

JellyGoose is a client for Jellyfin and Emby servers, which you or someone you know runs. In normal use it asks your server for your libraries and artwork, streams what you play, and reports your playback position so that Continue Watching works across your devices. That information goes to your server and stays there, under whatever policy that server keeps. We have no access to it.

Requests through Seerr

If you connect a Seerr, a server for requesting films and shows that runs beside your media server, the app signs in to it with your media server account and asks it what there is to request. The posters and pictures shown there come through your Seerr when its “Enable Image Caching” setting is on. When it's off, which is how Seerr comes, Seerr won't hand them out, and the app fetches them straight from The Movie Database (image.tmdb.org), as Seerr's own web pages do. Those requests carry nothing about you beyond what any request for a picture does: your device's internet address and which picture it wants. The Movie Database's own privacy policy covers them. To keep them from happening, turn on image caching in Seerr's General settings.

Children

The app collects no personal information from anyone, including children. What it can display is whatever is in your own library.

Changes

If this policy ever changes, the new version will appear on this page with a new date above.
